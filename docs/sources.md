## 1. The 14 Śiva Sūtras (with *it* markers)

Pāṇini’s system organizes the Sanskrit phonemes in 14 sūtras, each ending with a marker called '**it**'. This marker is not part of the sound inventory, but serves as a technical device.

**List of the 14 Sūtras** (with *it* marker shown at the end of each):

1. अ इ उ ण्  (a i u ṇ)
2. ऋ ऌ क्   (ṛ ḷ k)
3. ए ओ ङ्  (e o ṅ)
4. ऐ औ च्  (ai au c)
5. ह य व र ट्  (ha ya va ra ṭ)
6. ल ण्  (la ṇ)
7. ञ म ङ ण न म्  (ña ma ṅa ṇa na m)
8. झ भ ञ्  (jha bha ñ)
9. घ ढ ध ष्  (gha ḍha dha ṣ)
10. ज ब ग ड द श्  (ja ba ga ḍa da ś)
11. ख फ छ ठ थ च ट त व्  (kha pha cha ṭha tha ca ṭa ta v)
12. क प य्  (ka pa y)
13. श ष स र्  (śa ṣa sa r)
14. ह ल्  (ha l)

The *it* letter at the end of each sūtra is only a marker; it is not included in the set of phonemes for forming pratyāhāras.

## 2. Algorithm to Form Pratyāhāras

A **pratyāhāra** is a concise way to refer to a set/group of phonemes. To construct a pratyāhāra:

**Step 1:**  
Identify the **starting letter (phoneme)** and the **ending it-marker** (*it* letter) from the 14 sūtras.

**Step 2:**  
The pratyāhāra includes all letters from the **starting phoneme up to, but not including, the ending it-marker** specified in the corresponding sūtra.

**Step 3:**  
**The pratyāhāra is named by concatenating** the starting phoneme **and** the it-letter used as the endpoint. (For example: “aṇ”, “ik”, “al”.)

**Step 4:**  
In the technical literature, the “it” letter is subscripted or italicized to show it's a marker, not part of the group.

**Example Derivations:**

- **“ac”**: 'a' as starting sound, 'c' (from 4th sūtra) as it-marker.
  - All vowels: a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ, e, o, ai, au

- **“hal”**: 'h' as starting, 'l' (from 14th sūtra) as it-marker.
  - All consonants: All the letters from ha (in 5th sūtra) to the end (before 'l' marker).

- **“ik”**: i + k.
  - i, u, ṛ, ḷ

- **“yaṇ”**: y + ṇ.
  - y, v, r, l

### Etymology:
Pāṇini's sūtra **1.1.71**:  
"**स्वरितेनान्त्येन गुणवृद्धिपरिसंख्यानम्।**"  
Gives the rule: The pratyāhāra includes all phonemes between the starting sound and up to (not including) the it-marker.

**From the Kāśikā commentary**:  
"For example, in ‘ac’—from 'a' to before 'c' (i.e. includes up to au)."
